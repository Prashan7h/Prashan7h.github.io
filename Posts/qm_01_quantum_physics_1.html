8.04 Quantum Physics I — Cheat Sheet

Zwiebach, Mastering Quantum Mechanics Part I (Ch 1–11, 29) + MIT 8.04 notes (2016)

The course in 8 ideas

  1. State = complex wavefunction $\Psi$; $|\Psi|^2$ = probability density. Superpositions are allowed; overall phase is not physical.
  2. Dynamics: $i\hbar\partial_t\Psi=\hat H\Psi$. Energy eigenstates only pick up a phase $e^{-iEt/\hbar}$; every motion is a superposition of them.
  3. Observables = Hermitian operators. A measurement gives an eigenvalue with probability $|c_i|^2$ and leaves the eigenstate.
  4. Uncertainty comes from non-commuting operators ($[\hat x,\hat p]=i\hbar$) and is Fourier analysis: narrow in $x$ ⇔ wide in $k$.
  5. Bound states quantize energy: normalizability + matching conditions pick discrete $E_n$; state $n$ has $n-1$ nodes; $E_{\rm gs}>V_{\min}$.
  6. Scattering: unbound states; current conservation gives $R+T=1$; phase shifts give time delays; $\delta$ jumping by π marks a resonance.
  7. Oscillator: $\hat a,\hat a^\dagger$ give $E_n=\hbar\omega(n+\frac12)$ by algebra alone, the template for later courses.
  8. 3D central potentials: $\psi=\frac{u}{r}Y_{\ell m}$ turns 3D into a 1D problem with a centrifugal barrier. Hydrogen: $E_n=-13.6\,\text{eV}/n^2$, $n^2$-fold degenerate.

1 · Key features of QM L1 · Ch 1

  • Linearity. If $\Psi_1,\Psi_2$ are solutions, so is $\alpha\Psi_1+\beta\Psi_2$ — the Schrödinger eq. is linear, unlike Maxwell+matter or Newton in general.
  • Complex numbers are essential: $i\hbar\,\partial_t\Psi=\hat H\Psi$ has an explicit $i$; $\Psi$ cannot be real for all $t$.
  • Loss of determinism. Identical states can give different outcomes; QM predicts probabilities.
  • Superposition $|\psi\rangle=\alpha|A\rangle+\beta|B\rangle$: a measurement gives $A$ w.p. $|\alpha|^2$, $B$ w.p. $|\beta|^2$ — never "half of each". Adding a state to itself gives the same state ($|A\rangle+|A\rangle\sim|A\rangle$); overall phase is unphysical.
  • Entanglement. $|\psi\rangle=|u\rangle_1|u\rangle_2+|d\rangle_1|d\rangle_2$ can't be written as $|\cdot\rangle_1\otimes|\cdot\rangle_2$. Measuring 1 fixes 2 instantly; no signalling.
  • Photon polarization: a photon at angle $\alpha$ passes an $x$-polarizer w.p. $\cos^2\alpha$, then is $x$-polarized.
  • Making atoms possible. A classical orbiting electron radiates and spirals into the proton in $\sim10^{-11}$ s. In QM, squeezing the electron raises its kinetic energy ($p\sim\hbar/r$), so a stable ground state of size $a_0$ exists.

2 · Photon interferometry L2 · Ch 2

Photon state in two arms: $\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\\beta\end{pmatrix}$, $|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2=1$. A beam splitter is a unitary $2\times2$ matrix (columns orthonormal), e.g.

$$\text{BS1}=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\quad \text{BS2}=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad \text{BS2}\cdot\text{BS1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$$

Mach–Zehnder: photon in lower arm $\to$ output $D_0$ with prob 1 (interference). Blocking one arm destroys interference: each detector fires w.p. ¼, absorbed w.p. ½.

Elitzur–Vaidman bomb (live bomb = detector in one arm): explode ½, $D_0$ ¼ (inconclusive), $D_1$ ¼ → live bomb found without photon touching it. Dud: always $D_0$. Interaction-free measurement.

Phase shifter $e^{i\delta}$ in one arm: $P(D_0)$, $P(D_1)$ oscillate as $\cos^2(\delta/2)$, $\sin^2(\delta/2)$.

Toward perfect bomb detection (quantum Zeno). Put the photon in a cavity with a high-reflectivity splitter: each pass rotates its state by $\pi/2N$, so after $N$ passes it has moved fully to the other side (dud). A live bomb on that side "measures" the photon every pass and resets it. Survival: $$P_{\rm no\ boom}=\Big(\cos^2\frac{\pi}{2N}\Big)^N\simeq1-\frac{\pi^2}{4N}\to1$$ Photon still on the original side after $N$ passes ⇒ bomb is live, and it almost never explodes.

3 · Particle nature of light, wave nature of matter L3 · Ch 2

$$E=h\nu=\hbar\omega,\qquad p=\frac{h}{\lambda}=\hbar k,\qquad E^2=p^2c^2+m^2c^4$$ $$\textbf{de Broglie: }\lambda=\frac hp,\qquad \textbf{Photoelectric: } \hbar\omega = W + K_{\max}$$ $$\textbf{Compton: } \lambda_f-\lambda_i=\lambda_C(1-\cos\theta),\quad \lambda_C=\frac{h}{m_ec}\approx 2.43\,\text{pm}$$
  • Photoelectric: $K_{\max}$ depends on $\omega$ not intensity; threshold $\omega_0=W/\hbar$; no time lag. Intensity ∝ number of photons.
  • Compton: photon–electron elastic collision; derive with 4-momentum conservation. Shift is independent of $\lambda$; visible only for X/γ-rays.
  • Davisson–Germer: electron diffraction off nickel crystal confirms $\lambda=h/p$.
  • Nonrel. $\lambda=h/\sqrt{2mK}$; electron: $\lambda[\text{nm}]\approx 1.226/\sqrt{K[\text{eV}]}$.

4 · Free-particle waves; Galilean transformations L4 · Ch 2–3

$$\Psi(x,t)=e^{i(kx-\omega t)},\quad p=\hbar k,\quad E=\hbar\omega=\frac{\hbar^2k^2}{2m}$$ $$v_{\rm phase}=\frac{\omega}{k}=\frac{v}{2},\qquad v_{\rm group}=\frac{d\omega}{dk}\Big|_{k_0}=\frac{\hbar k_0}{m}=v$$
  • Choose $e^{i(kx-\omega t)}$ (not $\sin$/$\cos$, not $e^{-i(kx-\omega t)}$): a first-order-in-time equation must map a momentum state to itself; the convention $E>0 \Leftrightarrow e^{-iEt/\hbar}$.
  • de Broglie waves are not Galilean-invariant ($\lambda$ depends on frame) ⇒ $\Psi$ is not directly measurable; under a boost $\Psi$ picks up a $(x,t)$-dependent phase, $|\Psi|^2$ is invariant.
  • Stationary phase: a packet $\int\Phi(k)e^{i(kx-\omega(k)t)}dk$ with $\Phi$ peaked at $k_0$ has its peak where $\frac{d}{dk}[kx-\omega t]_{k_0}=0 \Rightarrow x=\omega'(k_0)t$.

5 · Schrödinger equation & wavefunction L5–6 · Ch 3

$$i\hbar\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}=\hat H\Psi=\Big(-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2}+V(x,t)\Big)\Psi$$ $$\hat p=\frac{\hbar}{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x},\qquad [\hat x,\hat p]=i\hbar,\qquad \hat E\leftrightarrow i\hbar\,\partial_t$$

Born rule: $\rho(x,t)=|\Psi(x,t)|^2$ is a probability density; $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\Psi|^2dx=1$. Requires $\Psi\to0$ faster than $|x|^{-1/2}$.

Conservation of normalization holds iff $\hat H$ is Hermitian ($V$ real).

$$\textbf{Current: } J=\frac{\hbar}{2mi}\Big(\Psi^*\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial x}-\Psi\frac{\partial\Psi^*}{\partial x}\Big)=\frac{\hbar}{m}\,\text{Im}\Big(\Psi^*\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial x}\Big)$$ $$\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial J}{\partial x}=0,\qquad \frac{dP_{ab}}{dt}=J(a,t)-J(b,t)$$ $$\textbf{3D: } \vec J=\frac{\hbar}{m}\text{Im}(\Psi^*\nabla\Psi),\quad \partial_t\rho+\nabla\!\cdot\!\vec J=0$$
  • Plane wave $Ae^{ikx}$: $J=|A|^2\,\hbar k/m$ = density × velocity. Real $\Psi$ (up to const phase) ⇒ $J=0$.
  • Units: $[\Psi]=L^{-1/2}$ (1D), $L^{-3/2}$ (3D); $[J]=T^{-1}$ (1D).

6 · Fourier, wavepackets & momentum space L7–8 · Ch 4–5

$$\Psi(x,0)=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\!\int\!\Phi(k)e^{ikx}dk,\qquad \Phi(k)=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\!\int\!\Psi(x,0)e^{-ikx}dx$$ $$\Psi(x,t)=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\Phi(k)\,e^{i(kx-\omega(k)t)}dk,\quad \omega=\frac{\hbar k^2}{2m}$$ $$\textbf{Parseval: }\int|\Psi(x)|^2dx=\int|\Phi(k)|^2dk \quad\big(=\textstyle\int|\tilde\Phi(p)|^2dp\big)$$ $$\tilde\Phi(p)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}\int\Psi(x)e^{-ipx/\hbar}dx,\qquad \Delta x\,\Delta k\gtrsim 1$$

Free evolution recipe: $\Psi(x,0)\xrightarrow{\text{FT}}\Phi(k)\xrightarrow{\times e^{-i\omega(k)t}}\xrightarrow{\text{FT}^{-1}}\Psi(x,t)$.

Shape change comes from the $k^2$ term in $\omega(k)$: expand $\omega(k)=\omega_0+\omega'(k-k_0)+\tfrac12\omega''(k-k_0)^2$. Packet distorts when $\tfrac{\hbar t}{2m}(\Delta k)^2\sim1$, i.e. $t\sim m(\Delta x)^2/\hbar$.

Gaussian packet $\Psi(x,0)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{1/4}\sqrt a}e^{-x^2/4a^2}$, $\Delta x(0)=a$, $\Delta p=\frac{\hbar}{2a}$ (minimum uncertainty): $$\Delta x(t)=a\sqrt{1+\Big(\frac{\hbar t}{2ma^2}\Big)^2},\qquad \Delta p \text{ constant}$$ FT of a Gaussian of width $a$ is a Gaussian of width $\propto1/a$. Momentum distribution of a free particle never changes.

Expectation values $\langle\hat Q\rangle=\int\Psi^*\hat Q\Psi\,dx$. E.g. $\langle\hat p\rangle=\int\Psi^*\frac\hbar i\Psi'dx=\int p|\tilde\Phi(p)|^2dp$.

$$\frac{d\langle\hat Q\rangle}{dt}=\frac i\hbar\big\langle[\hat H,\hat Q]\big\rangle+\Big\langle\frac{\partial\hat Q}{\partial t}\Big\rangle$$ Ehrenfest: $\displaystyle\frac{d\langle\hat x\rangle}{dt}=\frac{\langle\hat p\rangle}{m},\qquad \frac{d\langle\hat p\rangle}{dt}=-\Big\langle\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}\Big\rangle$

Note $\langle V'(x)\rangle\neq V'(\langle x\rangle)$ in general (equal for $V$ at most quadratic).

7 · Observables, Hermitian operators, uncertainty L9 · Ch 5

Inner product $(\phi,\psi)=\int\phi^*\psi\,dx$. Hermitian: $(\phi,\hat Q\psi)=(\hat Q\phi,\psi)$ ∀ $\phi,\psi$. $\hat x$, $\hat p$, $\hat H$ are Hermitian; $\hat p$'s hermiticity uses integration by parts + decay at ∞.

  • Expectation values of Hermitian operators are real.
  • Eigenvalues are real; eigenfunctions with distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal; eigenfunctions form a complete basis: $\Psi=\sum_i\alpha_i\psi_i$, $\alpha_i=(\psi_i,\Psi)$.
Measurement postulate. Measuring $\hat Q$ on $\Psi=\sum_i\alpha_i\psi_i$ yields eigenvalue $q_i$ with probability $|\alpha_i|^2$, and the state collapses to $\psi_i$. $\sum_i|\alpha_i|^2=1$, $\langle\hat Q\rangle=\sum_i q_i|\alpha_i|^2$.
$$(\Delta Q)^2=\langle\hat Q^2\rangle-\langle\hat Q\rangle^2=\big\langle(\hat Q-\langle\hat Q\rangle)^2\big\rangle=\int\!\big|(\hat Q-\langle \hat Q\rangle)\Psi\big|^2\ge0$$ $\Delta Q=0 \iff \Psi$ is an eigenstate of $\hat Q$. $$\Delta A\,\Delta B\ \ge\ \Big|\frac{1}{2i}\big\langle[\hat A,\hat B]\big\rangle\Big|\ \Rightarrow\ \Delta x\,\Delta p\ge\frac\hbar2$$ $$\textbf{Energy–time: }\Delta E\,\Delta t\ge\frac\hbar2,\qquad \Delta t\equiv\frac{\Delta Q}{|d\langle \hat Q\rangle/dt|}$$
Estimating ground energies: set $p\sim\hbar/\Delta x$ in $E(\Delta x)=\frac{\hbar^2}{2m\Delta x^2}+V(\Delta x)$ and minimize. Works for SHO, hydrogen ($a_0$), etc.

8 · Stationary states & the TISE L10 · Ch 6

$$\Psi(x,t)=\psi(x)e^{-iEt/\hbar},\qquad -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi''+V(x)\psi=E\psi$$ $$\Psi(x,t)=\sum_n c_n\,\psi_n(x)\,e^{-iE_nt/\hbar},\qquad c_n=\int\psi_n^*(x)\Psi(x,0)\,dx$$ $$\sum_n|c_n|^2=1,\qquad\langle \hat H\rangle=\sum_n|c_n|^2E_n\ \text{(time-indep.)}$$
  • Stationary states: $|\Psi|^2$ and all $\langle\hat Q\rangle$ (time-indep. $\hat Q$) are constant. $\Delta H=0$.
  • $E$ is real (normalizability). Normalizable solutions require $E>V_{\min}$.
  • Superposition of two energies: $|\Psi|^2$ oscillates at $\omega=(E_1-E_2)/\hbar$.

9 · Square wells L11 · Ch 6

Infinite well, $0<x<a$

$$\psi_n(x)=\sqrt{\frac2a}\sin\frac{n\pi x}{a},\qquad E_n=\frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2ma^2},\quad n=1,2,\dots$$

(Symmetric well $-a/2<x<a/2$: even $\cos$ states for odd $n$, odd $\sin$ states for even $n$.) $\psi_n$ has $n-1$ nodes; $\langle x\rangle=a/2$.

Finite well, $V=-V_0$ for $|x|<a$ ($-V_0<E<0$)

Inside $\psi\sim\cos kx$ or $\sin kx$, $k^2=\frac{2m(V_0+E)}{\hbar^2}$; outside $\psi\sim e^{-\kappa|x|}$, $\kappa^2=\frac{2m|E|}{\hbar^2}$. Let $\xi=ka$, $\eta=\kappa a$:

$$\xi^2+\eta^2=z_0^2\equiv\frac{2mV_0a^2}{\hbar^2}$$ $$\textbf{even: }\eta=\xi\tan\xi,\qquad\textbf{odd: }\eta=-\xi\cot\xi$$ Number of bound states $=\lceil 2z_0/\pi\rceil$; always ≥ 1 (even) bound state. $E=-V_0+\frac{\hbar^2\xi^2}{2ma^2}$.
Graphical solution: circle radius $z_0$ (here 5) vs. $\xi\tan\xi$ (blue, even) and $-\xi\cot\xi$ (red, odd). Each crossing = a bound state.

Deep well ($z_0\to\infty$): $\xi\to n\pi/2$, recovers infinite well of width $2a$. Shallow ($z_0\ll1$): one state, $\eta\approx z_0^2$.

10 · General properties of 1D bound states L12 · Ch 7

  1. No degenerate bound states in 1D (Wronskian argument).
  2. Energy eigenstates can be chosen real; a 1D bound state is real up to a constant phase.
  3. If $V(-x)=V(x)$: eigenstates can be chosen even/odd; bound states are automatically even or odd.
  4. Node theorem: the $n$-th bound state ($n=1$ ground) has $n-1$ nodes; ground state has none.
$$\frac{\psi''}{\psi}=\frac{2m}{\hbar^2}\big(V(x)-E\big)$$ $E>V$ (allowed): $\psi$ curves toward axis, oscillates. $E<V$ (forbidden): curves away, exponential.
Turning points $E=V(x)$: inflection points of $\psi$.

Slowly varying $V$: local momentum $p(x)=\sqrt{2m(E-V(x))}$, local wavelength $\lambda(x)=h/p(x)$. Where $E-V$ is larger: shorter wavelength and smaller amplitude, amplitude $\propto 1/\sqrt{p(x)}$ (classical particle spends less time there). Validity: $|d\lambda/dx|\ll 1$. Quantization estimate $\int_a^b k(x)\,dx\approx(n-\tfrac12)\pi$.

Shooting method: fix $\psi(0),\psi'(0)$ (by parity), integrate outward; $\psi$ blows up $\pm\infty$ unless $E$ = eigenvalue. Sign flip of the blow-up brackets an eigenvalue.

Four general tools (Ch 7.6–7.9)
  • Remove units: $x=Lu$, $E=\frac{\hbar^2}{mL^2}\varepsilon$ with $L$ built from the potential; only unit-free combinations (like $z_0$) matter.
  • Virial: in a stationary state $\langle[\hat H,\hat x\hat p]\rangle=0$ ⇒ $2\langle T\rangle=\langle xV'(x)\rangle$. For $V\propto|x|^k$: $2\langle T\rangle=k\langle V\rangle$ (SHO: $T=V=E/2$; Coulomb: $E=-\langle T\rangle=\frac12\langle V\rangle$).
  • Variational: $E_{\rm gs}\le\frac{\langle\psi|\hat H|\psi\rangle}{\langle\psi|\psi\rangle}$ for any trial $\psi$. Any 1D potential with $\int V\,dx<0$ (and $V\to0$) has a bound state.
  • Hellmann–Feynman: $\frac{\partial E_n}{\partial\lambda}=\langle\psi_n|\frac{\partial\hat H}{\partial\lambda}|\psi_n\rangle$. E.g. differentiate hydrogen $E_n$ w.r.t. $e^2$ to get $\langle1/r\rangle$.

11 · Delta-function & linear potentials L13 · Ch 6

$V=-\alpha\,\delta(x)$, $\alpha>0$: $$\psi'(0^+)-\psi'(0^-)=-\frac{2m\alpha}{\hbar^2}\psi(0)$$ $$\psi(x)=\sqrt\kappa\,e^{-\kappa|x|},\qquad \kappa=\frac{m\alpha}{\hbar^2},\qquad E=-\frac{m\alpha^2}{2\hbar^2}$$

Exactly one bound state. General: integrate TISE over $(-\epsilon,\epsilon)$ to get the jump in $\psi'$. Units: $[\delta(x)]=1/L$, so $[\alpha]=E\cdot L$. Scattering off $-\alpha\delta$: $T=\frac{1}{1+m\alpha^2/(2\hbar^2E)}$.

Linear potential $V=gx$ ($x>0$, wall at $0$)

Easiest in momentum space, where the TISE is first order: $i\hbar g\,\varphi'(p)=\big(E-\frac{p^2}{2m}\big)\varphi$ ⇒ $\varphi\propto\exp\!\big[-\frac{i}{\hbar g}\big(Ep-\frac{p^3}{6m}\big)\big]$. In position space this is an Airy function:

$$\psi(x)\propto\text{Ai}\Big(\frac{x-E/g}{\ell}\Big),\quad \ell=\Big(\frac{\hbar^2}{2mg}\Big)^{1/3},\qquad E_n=a_n\Big(\frac{\hbar^2g^2}{2m}\Big)^{1/3}$$ $a_n$ = $n$-th zero of Ai at $-a_n$: $2.338,\ 4.088,\ 5.521,\dots$ Uses: neutrons bouncing in gravity ($V=mgx$), WKB near turning points.

12 · Harmonic oscillator L13–15 · Ch 9

$$V=\tfrac12m\omega^2x^2,\qquad E_n=\hbar\omega\big(n+\tfrac12\big),\quad n=0,1,2,\dots$$ $$\psi_n(x)=\Big(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\Big)^{1/4}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^nn!}}H_n(u)\,e^{-u^2/2},\quad u=\frac xL,\ L=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}}$$

$H_0=1,\ H_1=2u,\ H_2=4u^2-2,\ H_3=8u^3-12u$. Parity $(-1)^n$. Series method: $\psi=h(u)e^{-u^2/2}$; series must terminate ⇒ $\mathcal E\equiv\frac{2E}{\hbar\omega}=2n+1$.

$\psi_0\dots\psi_3$ drawn at height $E_n$ inside $V=\frac12m\omega^2x^2$.

Algebraic method

$$\hat a=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\Big(\hat x+\frac{i\hat p}{m\omega}\Big),\quad \hat a^\dagger=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\Big(\hat x-\frac{i\hat p}{m\omega}\Big),\quad [\hat a,\hat a^\dagger]=1$$ $$\hat x=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}(\hat a+\hat a^\dagger),\qquad \hat p=i\sqrt{\frac{m\omega\hbar}{2}}(\hat a^\dagger-\hat a)$$ $$\hat H=\hbar\omega\big(\hat N+\tfrac12\big),\quad \hat N=\hat a^\dagger\hat a,\quad [\hat N,\hat a^\dagger]=\hat a^\dagger,\ [\hat N,\hat a]=-\hat a$$ $$\hat a^\dagger\varphi_n=\sqrt{n+1}\,\varphi_{n+1},\quad \hat a\varphi_n=\sqrt n\,\varphi_{n-1},\quad \varphi_n=\frac{(\hat a^\dagger)^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\varphi_0$$
  • Ground state: $\hat a\varphi_0=0\Rightarrow\varphi_0\propto e^{-m\omega x^2/2\hbar}$. Spectrum bounded below because $\langle\hat H\rangle\ge\frac12\hbar\omega$ (since $\langle \hat a^\dagger\hat a\rangle=\|\hat a\psi\|^2\ge0$).
  • $\hat a^\dagger$ raises $E$ by $\hbar\omega$; $\hat a$ lowers. $[\hat H,\hat a^\dagger]=\hbar\omega\,\hat a^\dagger$.
  • $\langle x\rangle_n=\langle p\rangle_n=0$; $\langle x^2\rangle_n=\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}(n+\frac12)$; $\langle p^2\rangle_n=m\hbar\omega(n+\frac12)$; $\Delta x\Delta p=\hbar(n+\frac12)$. Virial: $\langle T\rangle=\langle V\rangle=E_n/2$.
Trick: to get $\langle x^k\rangle$, $\langle p^k\rangle$, write in $\hat a,\hat a^\dagger$; only terms with equal numbers of $\hat a$ and $\hat a^\dagger$ survive in $\langle n|\cdot|n\rangle$.

13 · Step potential & 1D scattering L16–17 · Ch 8

$V=0$ for $x<0$, $V_0$ for $x>0$. Incident from left: $\psi=Ae^{ikx}+Be^{-ikx}$ ($x<0$), $Ce^{i\bar kx}$ ($x>0$).

$E>V_0$: $k=\frac{\sqrt{2mE}}\hbar$, $\bar k=\frac{\sqrt{2m(E-V_0)}}\hbar$ $$\frac BA=\frac{k-\bar k}{k+\bar k},\quad \frac CA=\frac{2k}{k+\bar k}$$ $$R=\Big|\frac BA\Big|^2=\Big(\frac{k-\bar k}{k+\bar k}\Big)^2,\qquad T=\frac{\bar k}{k}\Big|\frac CA\Big|^2=\frac{4k\bar k}{(k+\bar k)^2}$$ $R+T=1$ (current conservation, $J_A=J_B+J_C$).
Pitfall: $T\neq|C/A|^2$ — transmission is a ratio of currents, include the velocity factor $\bar k/k$. Classically $R=0$ for $E>V_0$; QM reflects even off a step down.
$E<V_0$: $\psi=Ce^{-\kappa x}$, $\kappa=\frac{\sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}}{\hbar}$ $$\frac BA=-\frac{\kappa+ik}{\kappa-ik}=-e^{2i\delta(E)}\ \Rightarrow\ R=1,\ T=0$$ Penetration depth $\sim1/\kappa$; reflected packet is delayed by $\Delta t=2\hbar\,\delta'(E)$.

Finite square well (scattering, $E>0$), $V=-V_0$ on width $2a$

$$\frac1T=1+\frac{V_0^2}{4E(E+V_0)}\sin^2(2k_2a),\quad k_2=\frac{\sqrt{2m(E+V_0)}}\hbar$$ Resonant transmission $T=1$ when $2k_2a=n\pi$: well width = integer # half-wavelengths (the would-be bound states of an infinite well of width $2a$).

Ramsauer–Townsend effect: slow electrons (~1 eV) pass almost freely through noble-gas atoms — resonant transmission; no classical explanation.

Barrier $V_0$ of width $2a$, $E<V_0$ (tunneling)

$$\frac1T=1+\frac{V_0^2}{4E(V_0-E)}\sinh^2(2\kappa a)\ \xrightarrow{\ \kappa a\gg1\ }\ T\approx\frac{16E(V_0-E)}{V_0^2}e^{-4\kappa a}$$ (For $E>V_0$ replace $\sinh\to\sin$, $\kappa\to\bar k$, $V_0-E\to E-V_0$.)

Wavepackets: peak of $\int\Phi(k)e^{i(kx-\omega t)}e^{i\phi(k)}$ shifted by $\phi'(k_0)$ — phases of $B/A$, $C/A$ give spatial shifts/time delays.

14 · Scattering on the half-line; phase shifts L18 · Ch 29

Wall at $x=0$, $V(x)=0$ for $x>R$. Free solution $\phi=\sin kx$. With potential, for $x>R$:

$$\psi(x)=e^{i\delta}\sin(kx+\delta)=\tfrac1{2i}\big({-e^{-ikx}}+e^{ikx+2i\delta}\big)$$ $$\psi=\phi+\psi_s,\qquad \psi_s=A_se^{ikx},\quad A_s=e^{i\delta}\sin\delta,\quad |A_s|^2=\sin^2\delta$$ $$\textbf{Time delay: }\ \Delta t=2\hbar\,\frac{d\delta}{dE}=\frac{2m}{\hbar k}\frac{d\delta}{dk}$$
  • $\delta(E)$ real (current conservation: outgoing $|{\cdot}|$ = incoming). $\delta$ and $\delta\pm\pi$ give the same $\psi$.
  • $\delta>0$: wave pulled in by $\delta/k$ — attractive. $\delta<0$: pushed out — repulsive.
  • Hard wall at $x=a$: $\delta=-ka$. $\Delta t<0$ possible (advance), bounded below.
  • Square well ($V=-V_0$, $0<x<a$), $k'=\sqrt{2m(E+V_0)}/\hbar$: match $A\sin k'a=e^{i\delta}\sin(ka+\delta)$ ⇒ $k\cot(ka+\delta)=k'\cot k'a$.
  • Unit-free delay $\frac1a\frac{d\delta}{dk}$ = delay / free transit time.

15 · Levinson's theorem & resonances L19 · Ch 29

$$\textbf{Levinson: }\ N_b=\frac1\pi\big(\delta(0)-\delta(\infty)\big)$$ Number of bound states = phase-shift excursion / π. Proof: add a regulator wall at $x=L$, count states.

Resonance: $\delta$ increases rapidly by $\sim\pi$ near $E_\alpha$; large time delay; $|A_s|^2=\sin^2\delta$ peaks at $\delta=\pi/2$. Physically: quasi-bound state trapped behind a barrier. Model:

$$\delta=\tan^{-1}\frac{\beta}{\alpha-k},\quad A_s=\frac{\beta}{\alpha-k-i\beta}\ \ (\text{pole at }k=\alpha-i\beta)$$ $$\textbf{Breit–Wigner: }\ |A_s|^2=\frac{\tfrac14\Gamma^2}{(E-E_\alpha)^2+\tfrac14\Gamma^2},\qquad \Gamma=\frac{2\alpha\beta\hbar^2}{m}$$ $\Gamma$ = full width at half max. Lifetime $\tau=\hbar/\Gamma$; peak delay $\Delta t=4\hbar/\Gamma$.
Resonance near $k=\alpha$: phase $\delta$ jumps by π (red) while $\sin^2\delta$ (blue) peaks, width ∝ β.

16 · QM in 3D; angular momentum L20–21 · Ch 10

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2\psi+V(\vec r)\psi=E\psi,\quad \hat{\vec p}=\frac\hbar i\nabla,\quad [\hat x_i,\hat p_j]=i\hbar\delta_{ij}$$ $$\hat{\vec L}=\hat{\vec r}\times\hat{\vec p},\qquad \hat L_i=\epsilon_{ijk}\hat x_j\hat p_k\ \ (\text{Hermitian, no ordering issue})$$ $$[\hat L_x,\hat L_y]=i\hbar\hat L_z\ \text{(+cyclic)},\quad [\hat L_i,\hat L_j]=i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}\hat L_k$$ $$[\hat L_i,\hat x_j]=i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}\hat x_k,\quad [\hat L_i,\hat p_j]=i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}\hat p_k,\quad [\hat L_i,\hat{\vec L}^2]=0$$
  • $\vec r^2$, $\vec p^2$, $\vec r\cdot\vec p$ are scalars: commute with every $\hat L_i$. Central $V(r)$ ⇒ $[\hat H,\hat L_i]=0$, $[\hat H,\hat L^2]=0$.
  • $\hat L_x,\hat L_y,\hat L_z$ mutually incompatible ⇒ choose CSCO $\{\hat H,\hat L^2,\hat L_z\}$.
  • $\hat L_i$ generate rotations (as $\hat p$ generates translations).

Radial equation

$$\psi_{E\ell m}=\frac{u_{E\ell}(r)}{r}Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi),\qquad -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}u''+V_{\rm eff}(r)\,u=E\,u$$ $$V_{\rm eff}=V(r)+\frac{\hbar^2\,\ell(\ell+1)}{2mr^2},\qquad \int_0^\infty|u|^2dr=1$$ $$u(0)=0,\qquad u\sim r^{\ell+1}\ \ (r\to0,\ V\text{ less singular than }1/r^2)$$
  • A 1D problem on the half-line $r>0$ with a hard wall at $0$ — 1D results apply (no degeneracy for fixed $\ell$; node counting).
  • Label states by $(n,\ell)$; each has $2\ell+1$ degeneracy from $m$. For fixed $\ell$, energy increases with # radial nodes. For fixed $n_r$, $E$ increases with $\ell$ (centrifugal barrier).
  • Large $r$ with $V\to0$, $E<0$: $u\sim e^{-\kappa r}$.
  • 3D isotropic SHO: $E=\hbar\omega(N+\tfrac32)$, deg. $\frac12(N+1)(N+2)$; $\ell=N,N-2,\dots$
  • Infinite spherical well (radius $a$), $\ell=0$: $u=\sin kr$, $E=\frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2ma^2}$. Finite spherical well, $\ell=0$ ↔ odd states of 1D well ⇒ bound state needs $z_0>\pi/2$.

17 · Hydrogen atom L21–22 · Ch 11

Two-body → one-body: $\vec P=\vec p_p+\vec p_e$, $\vec R=\frac{m_p\vec x_p+m_e\vec x_e}{M}$; relative $\vec x=\vec x_e-\vec x_p$, $\vec p=\frac{m_p\vec p_e-m_e\vec p_p}{M}$. $[x_i,p_j]=[X_i,P_j]=i\hbar\delta_{ij}$, cross commutators vanish. $\hat H=\frac{\hat P^2}{2M}+\frac{\hat p^2}{2\mu}+V(r)$, $\mu=\frac{m_em_p}{m_e+m_p}\approx m_e$. CM moves freely.

$V=-\dfrac{e^2}{r}$ (Gaussian; $e^2\to\frac{q^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0}$ in SI) $$a_0=\frac{\hbar^2}{me^2}\approx0.529\,\text{Å},\qquad \alpha=\frac{e^2}{\hbar c}\approx\frac{1}{137}$$ $$E_n=-\frac{e^2}{2a_0}\frac1{n^2}=-\frac{\alpha^2mc^2}{2n^2}=-\frac{13.6\,\text{eV}}{n^2}$$ $$n=N+\ell+1,\quad \ell=0,1,\dots,n-1,$$ $$\text{degeneracy}=\textstyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}(2\ell+1)=n^2$$

$N$ = number of radial nodes. Accidental degeneracy in $\ell$ (hidden symmetry: Runge–Lenz vector). $u\sim r^{\ell+1}e^{-r/na_0}\times$ polynomial of degree $N$.

20 · Problem-solving playbook

Bound-state problem: (1) sketch $V$, mark $E$ & turning points; (2) write $\psi$ in each region ($e^{\pm ikx}$ or $e^{\pm\kappa x}$), kill growing exponentials; (3) use parity if $V$ even; (4) match $\psi$, $\psi'$ (or the δ-jump) at boundaries; (5) get transcendental eq. → unit-free variables ($z_0$, $\xi$, $\eta$); (6) count nodes to label states.
Scattering problem: incident $Ae^{ikx}$ + reflected $Be^{-ikx}$ on left, only $Ce^{ik'x}$ on right; match; $R=|B/A|^2$, $T=\frac{k'}{k}|C/A|^2$; check $R+T=1$. Phases → time delays via $\Delta t=2\hbar\,d\delta/dE$.
Time evolution: expand $\Psi(x,0)=\sum c_n\psi_n$, attach $e^{-iE_nt/\hbar}$. For free particles, go through $\Phi(k)$. Probabilities of energies are $|c_n|^2$ — time-independent.
Sanity checks: units; $\hbar\to0$ classical limit; $R,T\in[0,1]$; $E_{\rm ground}>V_{\min}$; nodes = $n-1$; $\Delta x\Delta p\ge\hbar/2$; normalization $\int|\psi|^2=1$ (3D: $\int|u|^2dr=1$).
Common mistakes: forgetting $1/r$ in $\psi=u/r$ or $r^2$ in $d^3x=r^2dr\,d\Omega$; using $|C/A|^2$ for $T$; assuming $\langle V'(x)\rangle=V'(\langle x\rangle)$; normalizing with a time phase still attached to only some terms; treating $\hat L_x$ & $\hat L_z$ as simultaneously measurable; wrong sign in δ-function jump.

8.06 Quantum Physics III — Cheat Sheet

Zwiebach, Mastering Quantum Mechanics Part III (Ch 24–30) + MIT 8.06 notes (2018) · Gaussian units ($\alpha=e^2/\hbar c$)

The course in 8 ideas

  1. Almost nothing is exactly solvable. Start from a solved $\hat H^{(0)}$ and treat the rest as small. First order = average of the perturbation; second order = level repulsion; degenerate levels need the good basis.
  2. Real hydrogen: relativistic, spin-orbit and Darwin terms are $\alpha^2$ corrections that depend only on $n,j$. External fields: Zeeman (B) and Stark (E).
  3. EM fields enter through potentials: $\hat{\vec p}\to\hat{\vec p}-\frac qc\vec A$. Gauge invariance, flux quantization, Landau levels; the Pauli equation gives $g=2$.
  4. Semiclassical (WKB) when λ varies slowly: amplitude $\propto1/\sqrt p$, phase $\frac1\hbar\int p\,dx$; quantization $\oint p\,dx=2\pi\hbar(n+\frac12)$; tunneling $\sim e^{-2\theta}$.
  5. Time-dependent perturbations cause transitions. Into a continuum they give a constant rate (golden rule), hence exponential decay and line widths; light–atom physics and selection rules.
  6. Slow change ⇒ the system follows the instantaneous eigenstate, gaining a dynamical phase plus a geometric Berry phase. Fast crossings jump (Landau–Zener). Molecules: Born–Oppenheimer.
  7. Scattering is measured as cross sections. Low energy: a few partial waves (phase shifts). Weak $V$ or high energy: Born approximation, the Fourier transform of $V$.
  8. Identical particles: (anti)symmetrize. This gives Pauli exclusion, exchange effects and Fermi–Dirac / Bose–Einstein statistics.

1 · Time-independent perturbation theory notes 1 · Ch 25

$\hat H(\lambda)=\hat H^{(0)}+\lambda\,\delta\hat H$, $\lambda\in[0,1]$. Expand $E_n(\lambda)=E_n^{(0)}+\lambda E_n^{(1)}+\lambda^2E_n^{(2)}+\dots$, $|n\rangle=|n^{(0)}\rangle+\lambda|n^{(1)}\rangle+\dots$; match powers of λ. Convenient choice $\langle n^{(0)}|n^{(k)}\rangle=0$ for $k\ge1$.

Non-degenerate ($\delta H_{kn}\equiv\langle k^{(0)}|\delta\hat H|n^{(0)}\rangle$): $$E_n^{(1)}=\delta H_{nn},\qquad |n^{(1)}\rangle=\sum_{k\ne n}\frac{\delta H_{kn}}{E_n^{(0)}-E_k^{(0)}}|k^{(0)}\rangle$$ $$E_n^{(2)}=\sum_{k\ne n}\frac{|\delta H_{kn}|^2}{E_n^{(0)}-E_k^{(0)}}$$
  • Why: first order = average of the perturbation in the unperturbed state; second order = virtual mixing with other levels, weighted by 1/(energy gap).
  • $E^{(2)}_{\rm ground}<0$ always: levels repel at second order.
  • Validity: $|\delta H_{kn}|\ll|E_n^{(0)}-E_k^{(0)}|$. Series is often only asymptotic (e.g. $\alpha x^4$ with $\alpha<0$ has no bound states ⇒ zero radius of convergence).
  • Anharmonic $\delta H=\alpha\hat x^4$: $E_n^{(1)}=\frac{3\alpha\hbar^2}{4m^2\omega^2}(2n^2+2n+1)$. Use $\hat x\propto\hat a+\hat a^\dagger$.
Degenerate (level $E_n^{(0)}$ with subspace $V_N$, $N$-fold):
  1. Find the good basis: diagonalize $[\delta\hat H]_{V_N}$, the $N\times N$ matrix of $\delta\hat H$ inside $V_N$. Eigenvalues = $E^{(1)}$'s.
  2. Non-degenerate formulas then work in that basis (sums over states outside $V_N$).
  3. If $E^{(1)}$ doesn't lift degeneracy: diagonalize $M_{ij}=\sum_{p\notin V_N}\frac{\delta H_{ip}\delta H_{pj}}{E_n^{(0)}-E_p^{(0)}}$ inside the still-degenerate space (second-order lifting).
Why: only the good basis states connect smoothly to the exact $\lambda\ne0$ eigenstates; other bases make $\frac{\delta H_{kn}}{0}$ blow up.
Shortcut: find a Hermitian $\hat A$ with $[\hat A,\hat H^{(0)}]=[\hat A,\delta\hat H]=0$ whose eigenvalues are distinct on $V_N$ — its eigenstates are the good basis (e.g. $\hat L_z$, $\hat J^2$, parity).

Example — linear Stark effect ($n=2$ H, $\delta H=e\mathcal Ez$): only $\langle200|z|210\rangle=-3a_0$ is nonzero ⇒ good states $\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(|200\rangle\pm|210\rangle)$, $E^{(1)}=\mp3e\mathcal Ea_0$; $|21\pm1\rangle$ unshifted. Ground state: no linear Stark effect (parity).

Feynman–Hellmann: $\frac{dE_n}{d\lambda}=\langle n(\lambda)|\frac{d\hat H}{d\lambda}|n(\lambda)\rangle$. Variational: $E_{\rm gs}\le\langle\psi|\hat H|\psi\rangle$.

2 · Charged particles in EM fields Ch 24

$$\vec E=-\nabla\Phi-\frac1c\partial_t\vec A,\qquad \vec B=\nabla\times\vec A,\qquad \hat H=\frac{1}{2m}\Big(\hat{\vec p}-\frac qc\vec A\Big)^2+q\Phi$$ Gauge transformation $\vec A\to\vec A+\nabla\Lambda$, $\Phi\to\Phi-\frac1c\partial_t\Lambda$ is a symmetry if $\psi\to e^{iq\Lambda/\hbar c}\psi$. $\hat{\vec p}$ is canonical and gauge-dependent; the mechanical momentum $\hat{\vec\pi}=\hat{\vec p}-\frac qc\vec A=m\hat{\vec v}$ is physical: $$[\hat\pi_x,\hat\pi_y]=\frac{i\hbar q}{c}B_z,\qquad m\frac{d^2\hat{\vec x}}{dt^2}=q\Big[\vec E+\frac{1}{2c}\big(\hat{\vec v}\times\vec B-\vec B\times\hat{\vec v}\big)\Big]$$
  • Flux quantization (field on a torus): a consistent $\vec A$ exists only if $\Phi_B=n\,\Phi_0$, $\Phi_0=\frac{2\pi\hbar c}{q}$. Aharonov–Bohm: a loop around flux picks up phase $\frac{q}{\hbar c}\oint\vec A\cdot d\vec l=\frac{q\Phi_B}{\hbar c}$, even where $\vec B=0$.
Landau levels (uniform $B\hat z$, gauge $\vec A=Bx\,\hat y$): $p_y$ is conserved and $x$-motion is an oscillator centred at $x_0=\frac{cp_y}{qB}$: $$E=\hbar\omega_c\big(n+\tfrac12\big)+\frac{p_z^2}{2m},\qquad \omega_c=\frac{|q|B}{mc},\qquad \ell_B=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar c}{|q|B}}$$ Each level holds one state per flux quantum: $\frac{\#\text{states}}{\text{area}}=\frac{|q|B}{2\pi\hbar c}=\frac{B}{\Phi_0}$. (Basis of the quantum Hall effect.)

3 · Hydrogen fine structure & Zeeman notes 2 · Ch 24.6–7, 25.6–8

$$E_n^{(0)}=-\frac{e^2}{2a_0n^2}=-\frac{\alpha^2mc^2}{2n^2},\quad a_0=\frac{\hbar^2}{me^2}\approx0.53\,\text{Å},\quad \alpha=\frac{e^2}{\hbar c}\approx\frac1{137}$$ Scales: Bohr $\alpha^2mc^2$ ≫ fine $\alpha^4mc^2$ ≫ Lamb $\alpha^5mc^2$ ~ hyperfine $\alpha^4mc^2\,\frac{m}{m_p}$. $v/c\sim\alpha$.

Pauli equation: $\hat H=\frac{(\vec\sigma\cdot\hat{\vec\pi})^2}{2m}+V$, $\hat{\vec\pi}=\hat{\vec p}-\frac qc\vec A$, and $(\vec\sigma\cdot\vec\pi)^2=\vec\pi^2-\frac{q\hbar}{c}\vec\sigma\cdot\vec B$ ⇒ predicts $g=2$. Dirac: $\hat H=c\,\vec\alpha\cdot\hat{\vec p}+\beta mc^2+V$; expanding in $v/c$ gives three corrections:

$$\delta\hat H_{\rm rel}=-\frac{\hat p^4}{8m^3c^2},\qquad \delta\hat H_{\rm SO}=\frac{e^2}{2m^2c^2}\frac{1}{r^3}\hat{\vec S}\cdot\hat{\vec L},\qquad \delta\hat H_{\rm D}=\frac{\pi\hbar^2e^2}{2m^2c^2}\delta^3(\vec r)$$ $$E_{\rm rel}^{(1)}=-\frac{(E_n^{(0)})^2}{2mc^2}\Big[\frac{4n}{\ell+\frac12}-3\Big],\qquad E_{\rm D}^{(1)}=\frac{\alpha^4mc^2}{2n^3}\ \ (\ell=0\text{ only})$$ $$E_{\rm SO}^{(1)}=\frac{(E_n^{(0)})^2}{mc^2}\,\frac{n\,[\,j(j+1)-\ell(\ell+1)-\frac34\,]}{\ell(\ell+\frac12)(\ell+1)}\quad(\ell\ne0)$$ $$\boxed{E^{(1)}_{\rm fs}=-\frac{\alpha^4mc^2}{2n^4}\Big[\frac{n}{j+\frac12}-\frac34\Big]}$$
  • Why each term: rel = next term of $\sqrt{p^2c^2+m^2c^4}$; SO = electron's magnetic moment in the proton's field seen in its rest frame (with Thomas ½); Darwin = electron position smeared over $\sim\hbar/mc$ (Zitterbewegung), only felt at $r=0$ ⇒ s-states.
  • $\delta H_{\rm rel}$ commutes with $\hat{\vec L}$; $\delta H_{\rm SO}$ doesn't commute with $L_z,S_z$ ⇒ use the coupled basis $|n\ell jm_j\rangle$ (good basis!).
  • Result depends only on $n,j$: $2s_{1/2}$ & $2p_{1/2}$ stay degenerate (split by Lamb shift, QED). Levels with larger $j$ lie higher.
  • $\langle\frac1r\rangle=\frac{1}{n^2a_0}$, $\langle\frac1{r^2}\rangle=\frac{1}{n^3(\ell+\frac12)a_0^2}$, $\langle\frac1{r^3}\rangle=\frac{1}{n^3\ell(\ell+\frac12)(\ell+1)a_0^3}$, $|\psi_{n00}(0)|^2=\frac{1}{\pi n^3a_0^3}$.
Zeeman effect: $\delta\hat H_Z=\frac{e}{2mc}(\hat{\vec L}+2\hat{\vec S})\cdot\vec B=\frac{\mu_B}{\hbar}B(\hat L_z+2\hat S_z)$, $\mu_B=\frac{e\hbar}{2mc}\approx5.79\times10^{-9}$ eV/G.
Weak field ($B\ll B_{\rm int}$, treat after fine structure): basis $|n\ell jm_j\rangle$, $$E^{(1)}_Z=\mu_BB\,g_J\,m_j,\qquad g_J=1+\frac{j(j+1)-\ell(\ell+1)+\frac34}{2j(j+1)}$$ removes all degeneracies. Strong field (Paschen–Back): basis $|n\ell m_\ell m_s\rangle$, $E=E_n^{(0)}+\mu_BB(m_\ell+2m_s)$, then fine structure as a perturbation. Intermediate: diagonalize $\delta H_{\rm fs}+\delta H_Z$ in the $n$ shell.

Projection lemma (why $g_J$): within a $j$-multiplet, any vector operator acts as $\langle\hat{\vec V}\rangle=\frac{\langle(\hat{\vec V}\cdot\hat{\vec J})\rangle}{\hbar^2j(j+1)}\langle\hat{\vec J}\rangle$ — only the part of $\vec S$ along $\vec J$ survives time-averaging.

4 · WKB / semiclassical approximation notes 3 · Ch 26

$$p(x)=\sqrt{2m(E-V(x))},\qquad \lambda(x)=\frac{2\pi\hbar}{p(x)}$$ $$\textbf{allowed: }\ \psi\simeq\frac{C}{\sqrt{p(x)}}\exp\Big(\pm\frac i\hbar\int^xp(x')dx'\Big)$$ $$\textbf{forbidden: }\ \psi\simeq\frac{C}{\sqrt{|p(x)|}}\exp\Big(\pm\frac1\hbar\int^x|p(x')|dx'\Big)$$

Derivation: $\psi=e^{iS(x)/\hbar}$, $S=S_0+\hbar S_1+\dots$ ⇒ $S_0'^2=p^2$ (Hamilton–Jacobi, classical), $S_1=\frac i2\ln p$ (amplitude $\propto p^{-1/2}$: $|\psi|^2\propto$ time spent at $x$). Valid when $\lambda$ changes slowly: $\big|\frac{d\lambda}{dx}\big|\ll2\pi$, i.e. $\hbar|p'|\ll p^2$. Fails at turning points ($p=0$).

Connection formulas ($k=p/\hbar$, $\kappa=|p|/\hbar$). Turning point $a$, allowed on left, forbidden on right: $$\frac{2}{\sqrt k}\cos\Big(\int_x^ak\,dx'-\frac\pi4\Big)\ \Longleftarrow\ \frac1{\sqrt\kappa}\exp\Big(-\int_a^x\kappa\,dx'\Big)$$ $$-\frac{1}{\sqrt k}\sin\Big(\int_x^ak\,dx'-\frac\pi4\Big)\ \Longrightarrow\ \frac1{\sqrt\kappa}\exp\Big(\int_a^x\kappa\,dx'\Big)$$ (Mirror image for forbidden-left at $b$: $\int_b^x$.) Arrows: which direction the matching is reliable. Derived by matching to Airy functions of the linearized potential $V\approx V(a)+g(x-a)$.
Bohr–Sommerfeld quantization: $$\int_a^bk(x)\,dx=\Big(n+\tfrac12\Big)\pi,\quad n=0,1,\dots\quad(\text{two soft turning points})$$ One hard wall: $(n+\frac34)\pi$; two hard walls: $(n+1)\pi$. Exact for the SHO. Equivalent to $\oint p\,dx=2\pi\hbar(n+\frac12)$: each state occupies phase-space area $2\pi\hbar$.
Tunneling through a barrier ($a<x<b$ forbidden): $$T\simeq e^{-2\theta},\qquad \theta=\int_a^b\kappa(x)\,dx=\frac1\hbar\int_a^b\sqrt{2m(V-E)}\,dx\ \ (\theta\gg1)$$ Square barrier check: $T\simeq\frac{16E}{V_0}\big(1-\frac{E}{V_0}\big)e^{-2\kappa L}$ — WKB gets the exponent, not the prefactor. Uses: α-decay (Gamow), field emission, fusion.
Double well (Ch 26.7): each well alone has a level $E_0$ with classical frequency ω. Tunneling splits it into an even state (lower) and an odd state (higher): $$\Delta E=E_{\rm odd}-E_{\rm even}\simeq\frac{\hbar\omega}{\pi}e^{-\theta},\qquad \theta=\frac1\hbar\int_{\rm barrier}\!|p|\,dx$$ A particle starting in one well tunnels back and forth with period $2\pi\hbar/\Delta E$ (ammonia inversion).

5 · Time-dependent perturbation theory notes 4 · Ch 27

$\hat H(t)=\hat H^{(0)}+\delta\hat H(t)$ has no energy eigenstates; expand in eigenstates of $\hat H^{(0)}$ and ask for transition probabilities.

Interaction picture: $|\tilde\Psi(t)\rangle=e^{i\hat H^{(0)}t/\hbar}|\Psi(t)\rangle$ removes the trivial $H^{(0)}$ evolution: $$i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}|\tilde\Psi\rangle=\widetilde{\delta H}(t)|\tilde\Psi\rangle,\qquad \widetilde{\delta H}=e^{i\hat H^{(0)}t/\hbar}\,\delta\hat H\,e^{-i\hat H^{(0)}t/\hbar}$$ $$|\Psi(t)\rangle=\sum_nc_n(t)e^{-iE_nt/\hbar}|n\rangle,\qquad i\hbar\,\dot c_n=\sum_m\delta H_{nm}(t)\,e^{i\omega_{nm}t}c_m$$ with $\omega_{nm}=(E_n-E_m)/\hbar$. $$c_f^{(1)}(t)=\frac{1}{i\hbar}\int_{t_0}^t\delta H_{fi}(t')\,e^{i\omega_{fi}t'}dt',\qquad P_{f\leftarrow i}=|c^{(1)}_f|^2$$ (1st order, starting in $|i\rangle$).

Valid while $P\ll1$. Exact two-level (NMR) solution reduces to this at small $t$. Note $P_{f\leftarrow i}=P_{i\leftarrow f}$ at first order.

Constant perturbation $V$ turned on at $t=0$: $$P_{f\leftarrow i}(t)=\frac{4|V_{fi}|^2}{\hbar^2}\frac{\sin^2(\omega_{fi}t/2)}{\omega_{fi}^2}$$ Harmonic $\delta H=2H'\cos\omega t$: same with $\omega_{fi}\to\omega_{fi}\mp\omega$ ⇒ absorption at $E_f=E_i+\hbar\omega$, stimulated emission at $E_f=E_i-\hbar\omega$.
$\sin^2(\omega t/2)/\omega^2$: peak height ∝ $t^2$, width ∝ $1/t$, area ∝ $t$ ⇒ for a continuum of final states the probability grows linearly in $t$ → constant rate.
Fermi's Golden Rule (transitions into a continuum): $$w=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\,|V_{fi}|^2\,\rho(E_f),\quad E_f=E_i\qquad\Big(\text{harmonic: }|H'_{fi}|^2,\ E_f=E_i\pm\hbar\omega\Big)$$ Uses $\frac{\sin^2(xt/2)}{x^2}\to\frac{\pi t}{2}\delta(x)$. Free particle in box $L^3$: $\rho(E)\,d\Omega=\frac{L^3}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{mk}{\hbar^2}d\Omega$ (L³ cancels against normalization). Valid for $\frac{2\pi\hbar}{\Delta E}\ll t\ll\frac1w$.

Ionization of H: golden rule with plane-wave final electron; $dw\propto\cos^2\theta\,d\Omega$.

Decay of a discrete state into a continuum (Ch 27.8): beyond first order the golden-rule rate gives true exponential decay, $$|c_i(t)|^2=e^{-\Gamma t/\hbar},\qquad \Gamma=2\pi|V|^2\rho,\qquad \tau=\hbar/\Gamma$$ with a Lorentzian line of full width Γ (energy–time: $\Gamma\tau=\hbar$).
Helium autoionization: doubly excited states (e.g. $2s^2$) lie above the $\text{He}^++e^-$ threshold. Electron–electron repulsion couples them to that continuum, so they decay by ejecting an electron.

Light and atoms

Dipole approximation ($\lambda\gg a_0$): $\delta\hat H=-\hat{\vec d}\cdot\vec{\mathcal E}(t)$, $\hat{\vec d}=q\hat{\vec r}$. Isotropic, incoherent light with energy density $U(\omega)$: $$R_{a\leftarrow b}=\frac{4\pi^2}{3\hbar^2}|\vec d_{ab}|^2U(\omega_{ba})=B_{ab}U(\omega_{ba})$$ Einstein's argument (equilibrium with Planck $U=\frac{\hbar\omega^3}{\pi^2c^3}\frac{1}{e^{\beta\hbar\omega}-1}$) needs spontaneous emission: $$B_{ab}=B_{ba},\qquad \frac{A}{B}=\frac{\hbar\omega^3}{\pi^2c^3}\ \Rightarrow\ A=\frac{4\omega^3}{3\hbar c^3}|\vec d_{ab}|^2,\qquad \tau=\frac1A$$ Rates from different channels add: $\tau^{-1}=\sum_iA_i$. Spontaneous = emission stimulated by vacuum fluctuations. H $2p\to1s$: τ ≈ 1.6 ns.
Selection rules (E1): $\langle n'\ell'm'|\hat{\vec r}|n\ell m\rangle\ne0$ only if $\Delta\ell=\pm1$, $\Delta m=0,\pm1$ ($z$: $\Delta m=0$; $x\pm iy$: $\Delta m=\pm1$). Parity must flip. ⇒ $2s$ is metastable (decays by 2 photons, ~0.1 s).

6 · Adiabatic approximation notes 6 · Ch 28

Classical: slowly varying oscillator conserves the action $I=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint p\,dq=E/\omega$ (adiabatic invariant). Quantum analogue: $n$ in $E=\hbar\omega(n+\frac12)$ stays fixed. Generally, slow changes preserve $\oint p\,dq=2\pi\hbar(n+\frac12)$, so quantum numbers are adiabatic invariants.

Adiabatic theorem. Instantaneous eigenstates $\hat H(t)|\psi_n(t)\rangle=E_n(t)|\psi_n(t)\rangle$. If $\hat H$ changes slowly and $E_n$ stays non-degenerate, a system starting in $|\psi_n(0)\rangle$ stays in $|\psi_n(t)\rangle$: $$|\Psi(t)\rangle\simeq e^{i\theta_n(t)}e^{i\gamma_n(t)}|\psi_n(t)\rangle,$$$$\theta_n=-\frac1\hbar\int_0^tE_n(t')dt',\qquad \gamma_n=\int_0^t\nu_n\,dt',\quad \nu_n=i\langle\psi_n|\dot\psi_n\rangle$$ Condition: $\hbar\,|\langle\psi_m|\dot{\hat H}|\psi_n\rangle|\ll(E_n-E_m)^2$ for all $m\ne n$ — rate of change slow compared to the gap.
Berry (geometric) phase for a closed loop $C$ in parameter space $\vec R$: $$\gamma_n[C]=\oint_C\vec{\mathcal A}_n\cdot d\vec R,\qquad \vec{\mathcal A}_n(\vec R)=i\langle\psi_n(\vec R)|\nabla_R\psi_n(\vec R)\rangle$$ $$=\int_S\vec{\mathcal D}_n\cdot d\vec a,\qquad \vec{\mathcal D}_n=\nabla_R\times\vec{\mathcal A}_n\ (\text{Berry curvature})$$ Depends only on the path's geometry, not on how fast it is traversed. Gauge: $|\psi_n\rangle\to e^{i\beta(R)}|\psi_n\rangle$ shifts $\vec{\mathcal A}\to\vec{\mathcal A}-\nabla\beta$; closed-loop γ invariant (mod 2π) ⇒ physical.
  • $\gamma_n=0$ if $\psi_n$ can be chosen real, or if there is only one parameter.
  • Spin in slowly rotating $\vec B$: $\gamma_\pm=\mp\frac12\Omega$, Ω = solid angle traced by $\hat B$. (Spin $s$: $\gamma=-m_s\Omega$.) Berry curvature = monopole field $\mp\frac{\hat r}{2r^2}$ at the degeneracy $B=0$.
Landau–Zener. Diabatic levels cross linearly, $E_1-E_2=\alpha t$, coupling $H_{12}$ opens a gap $2|H_{12}|$. Probability of a non-adiabatic jump (staying on the diabatic line): $$P_{\rm jump}=\exp\Big(-\frac{2\pi|H_{12}|^2}{\hbar|\alpha|}\Big)$$ Slow sweep ($\hbar|\alpha|\ll|H_{12}|^2$) ⇒ adiabatic, follows the curve; fast ⇒ jumps.
Avoided crossing: diabatic levels (dashed) vs adiabatic levels (solid), gap $2|H_{12}|$.
Born–Oppenheimer (molecules): electrons fast, nuclei slow ($m/M\ll1$). Solve electrons at fixed nuclear positions $\vec R$ → $E_{\rm el}(\vec R)$ is the potential for nuclear motion. Scales $E_{\rm el}:E_{\rm vib}:E_{\rm rot}\sim1:(m/M)^{1/2}:(m/M)$. Nuclear $\hat P\to\hat P-\vec{\mathcal A}$ (Berry connection from the electrons). H₂⁺: trial $\psi_\pm\propto\psi_{1s}(r_1)\pm\psi_{1s}(r_2)$; even (bonding) combination gives a bound molecule, odd (antibonding) doesn't.

7 · Scattering (3D, elastic, spinless) notes 7 · Ch 30

$E=\frac{\hbar^2k^2}{2m}$ ($m$ = reduced mass). Far from the target ($r\gg a$): $$\psi(\vec r)\simeq e^{ikz}+f_k(\theta,\phi)\frac{e^{ikr}}{r},\qquad \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=|f_k(\theta,\phi)|^2,\qquad \sigma=\int|f_k|^2d\Omega$$ $d\sigma$ = (scattered particles/time into $d\Omega$)/(incident flux) — an area.
Partial waves (central $V$): $e^{ikz}=\sum_\ell i^\ell(2\ell+1)j_\ell(kr)P_\ell(\cos\theta)$. Each $\ell$ outgoing wave acquires $e^{2i\delta_\ell}$: $$f_k(\theta)=\frac1k\sum_{\ell=0}^\infty(2\ell+1)\,e^{i\delta_\ell}\sin\delta_\ell\,P_\ell(\cos\theta)$$ $$\sigma=\frac{4\pi}{k^2}\sum_\ell(2\ell+1)\sin^2\delta_\ell,\qquad \sigma_\ell\le\frac{4\pi}{k^2}(2\ell+1)\ \ (\text{unitarity bound})$$ $$\textbf{Optical theorem: }\ \sigma=\frac{4\pi}{k}\,\text{Im}\,f_k(0)$$ Outside the potential: $u_\ell\sim\sin(kr-\frac{\ell\pi}{2}+\delta_\ell)$, $R_\ell\propto\cos\delta_\ell\,j_\ell(kr)-\sin\delta_\ell\,n_\ell(kr)$.
  • Why few partial waves: classical impact parameter $b\sim\ell/k$; if $b>a$ no scattering ⇒ $\ell_{\max}\sim ka$. Low energy ($ka\ll1$): $\delta_\ell\sim(ka)^{2\ell+1}$, s-wave dominates, isotropic.
  • Computing δℓ: match log-derivative $\beta=R_\ell'/R_\ell$ at $r=a$: $\tan\delta_\ell=\frac{kj_\ell'(ka)-\beta j_\ell(ka)}{kn_\ell'(ka)-\beta n_\ell(ka)}$.
  • Hard sphere: $\tan\delta_\ell=\frac{j_\ell(ka)}{n_\ell(ka)}$, $\delta_0=-ka$; low energy $\sigma\to4\pi a^2$ (4× geometric cross-section!). Scattering length: $\delta_0\simeq-ka_s$, $\sigma\to4\pi a_s^2$.
  • Resonance: $\delta_\ell$ passes through $\pi/2$ ⇒ $\sigma_\ell$ hits the unitarity bound; Breit–Wigner shape.
Integral equation: $(\nabla^2+k^2)\psi=U\psi$, $U=\frac{2m}{\hbar^2}V$, Green's function $G(\vec r)=-\frac{e^{ikr}}{4\pi r}$: $$\psi(\vec r)=e^{i\vec k\cdot\vec r}+\int G(\vec r-\vec r')U(\vec r')\psi(\vec r')d^3r'$$$$f=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int e^{-i\vec k'\cdot\vec r'}U(\vec r')\psi(\vec r')d^3r'$$ Born approximation (replace ψ by the incident plane wave), $\vec K=\vec k'-\vec k$, $K=2k\sin\frac\theta2$: $$f_B(\theta)=-\frac{m}{2\pi\hbar^2}\int e^{-i\vec K\cdot\vec r}V(\vec r)\,d^3r\ \xrightarrow{\text{central}}\ -\frac{2m}{\hbar^2K}\int_0^\infty rV(r)\sin(Kr)\,dr$$ $f_B$ ∝ Fourier transform of $V$ at momentum transfer $\hbar\vec K$. Valid for weak $V$ or high energy ($|V|a\ll\hbar v$).

Yukawa $V=\beta\frac{e^{-\mu r}}{r}$: $f_B=-\frac{2m\beta}{\hbar^2(\mu^2+K^2)}$. $\mu\to0$ gives Rutherford: $\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=\frac{(Z_1Z_2e^2)^2}{16E^2\sin^4(\theta/2)}$ (exact classically & quantum).

1D half-line scattering (phase shifts, time delay $2\hbar\delta'(E)$, Levinson, Breit–Wigner) is book Ch 29 and on the 8.04 sheet §14–15.

8 · Identical particles notes 8 · Ch 21

Identical = same intrinsic properties ⇒ no experiment distinguishes them. Classically you can still follow trajectories; quantum mechanically you can't once wavefunctions overlap ⇒ exchange degeneracy: $|a\rangle_1|b\rangle_2$ and $|b\rangle_1|a\rangle_2$ (and all combos) describe the same physics — ambiguity.

Permutation operators: $\hat P_{21}|u\rangle_1|v\rangle_2=|v\rangle_1|u\rangle_2$; $\hat P_{21}^2=1$, Hermitian & unitary. $N$ particles: $N!$ permutations $\hat P_\alpha$, parity $\epsilon_\alpha=\pm1$. $$\hat S=\frac1{N!}\sum_\alpha\hat P_\alpha,\qquad \hat A=\frac1{N!}\sum_\alpha\epsilon_\alpha\hat P_\alpha\qquad(\text{projectors})$$ Symmetrization postulate: states of identical particles are totally symmetric (bosons, integer spin) or totally antisymmetric (fermions, half-integer spin). Observables commute with all $\hat P_\alpha$.
  • Two particles: $\psi_\pm=\frac1{\sqrt2}[\psi_a(1)\psi_b(2)\pm\psi_b(1)\psi_a(2)]$. Fermions: $N$-particle state = Slater determinant $\frac{1}{\sqrt{N!}}\det[\psi_{a_i}(j)]$ ⇒ Pauli exclusion.
  • Exchange effect: $\langle(x_1-x_2)^2\rangle_\pm=\langle x^2\rangle_a+\langle x^2\rangle_b-2\langle x\rangle_a\langle x\rangle_b\mp2|\langle x\rangle_{ab}|^2$ — bosons bunch, fermions avoid each other (only if wavefunctions overlap).
  • Space ⊗ spin ($V\otimes W$): electrons need total antisymmetry ⇒ symmetric space × spin singlet, or antisymmetric space × triplet. Helium: para (singlet) vs ortho (triplet); triplet lower (electrons kept apart ⇒ less repulsion).
  • Occupation numbers $|n_1,n_2,\dots\rangle$: fully specify symmetric/antisymmetric states. Fermions $n_i\in\{0,1\}$; bosons $n_i=0,1,2,\dots$
Counting $N$ particles in $g$ single-particle states: $$\text{distinguishable: }g^N,\qquad \text{fermions: }\binom gN,\qquad \text{bosons: }\binom{N+g-1}{N}$$ Maximizing entropy gives mean occupancy $$\bar n(\epsilon)=\frac{1}{e^{(\epsilon-\mu)/kT}+1}\ (\text{FD}),\quad \frac{1}{e^{(\epsilon-\mu)/kT}-1}\ (\text{BE}),\quad e^{-(\epsilon-\mu)/kT}\ (\text{MB})$$
